# Saving Mowgli

«Saving Mowgli» is a short duration role-playing game (half an hour or less) in which you play as Mowgli’s animal friends. To play, you will need paper, a pencil, and two eight-sided dice.

Mowgli, the human cub from "The Jungle Book," has woken up and gone out alone to explore the jungle. Players take on the roles of his animal friends, who must prevent him from falling into any of the jungle’s many dangers—and, if possible, help him learn a life lesson. 

## Rules XXX

When your players attempt **something risky, something that could fail or that directly affects Mowgli, they must roll a 1d8 and check the result.

* **1** Failure with a serious consequence for Mowgli
* **2-4** Failure with a serious consequence for the player, or a mild one for Mowgli
* **5-6** Success with a mild consequence for either the player or Mowgli
* **7-8** Success with no consequences for anyone.

If an elephant tries to move a fallen tree, no roll is required. If it wants to knock down the central column of a ruined temple, a roll is necessary.

If you have **advantage** on a roll, you **roll 2d8 and keep the one that suits you best** (not necessarily the highest). If you have **disadvantage, you also roll 2d8 and keep the one that suits you best**, but if you choose the higher of the two dice, it will always carry a serious consequence, even if the result is a success.

If you don't know why you can choose the outcome that suits you best, it’s because you might need Mowgli to get into trouble or suffer some harm in order for him to learn an important, life lesson. For example, if he’s stealing honey, you might want him to get stung by a few bees so he learns his lesso, and then you can show him that going underwater is the best way to get away from the bees. If he’s escaping from Shere Khan, you certainly wouldn’t want him to suffer any harm, as that could be fatal.

If a player rolls with advantage against an animal that has an applicable advantage, the advantage is canceled out. If a player rolls with disadvantage against an animal that has an applicable disadvantage, the disadvantage is canceled out. In any other scenario, the roll neither gains nor loses advantage or disadvantage.

### Consequences

The **consequences for a PC** can take many forms, such as making an enemy, injuries or falling into a trap, but they must be immediate, given the short duration of the game sessions. At the GM’s discretion, certain consequences, such as injuries, could impose disadvantage on dice rolls.

The **consequences for Mowgli** should get him new troubles or amplify his existing problems. If too many consequences pile up, he could die, and the adventure would come to an end.

## Animals

The first step in creating your character is **choosing their species**. Species cannot be duplicated, except in cases such as wolves or monkeys, which typically live in packs. In such instances, there may be two animals of the same species.

Each animal has **two attributes** (strength, intelligence, dexterity and knack) in which it has an **advantage**, and one **situation** in which it has a **disadvantage**.

* **Bear:** Advantage in **strength and knack**. Disadvantage when there is **delicious food**.
* **Panther:** Advantage in **dexterity and intelligence**. Disadvantage in **open spaces**.
* **Tiger:** Advantage in **dexterity and strength**. Disadvantage there is **fire**.
* **Python:** Advantage in **strength and dexterity**. Disadvantage **by night or after eating**.
* **Wolf:** Advantage in **dexterity and intelligence**. Disadvantage without **their pack near**.
* **Squirrel:** Advantage in **dexterity and knack**. Disadvantage with **big things**.
* **Orangutan:** Advantage in **strength and knack**. Disadvantage near **humans and their tools**.
* **Monkey:** Advantage in **dexterity and knack**. Disadvantage without **their pack near**.
* **Elephant:** Advantage in **strength and intelligence**. Disadvantage in **enclosed spaces**.

These are just examples. You can create any jungle animals you like, using these examples as a guide.

After that, you will need to choose a **name** and your **pronouns**, and describe **how you met Mowgli** as well as the **life lesson you teach him** during that first encounter.

## The law of the jungle

The Law of the Jungle, which even the mighty Shere Khan obeys at times, establishes that:

* The pack protects its own.
* Killing for fun or sport is forbidden.
* Favors must be returned.
* Humans must not be killed.

At the start of each adventure, the GM may, if they wish, introduce an additional rule of their choosing. This is intended to help the adventure progress properly. Any such new law remains in effect for subsequent adventures.

Your players may choose to **break the laws** of the jungle, but doing so must always **have consequences**, and the players should be warned before.

## Mowgli

Mowgli does not follow the Law of the Jungle; he simply follows the teachings he has received from his animal friends.

Keep in mind that Mowgli begins his adventures with as many vital lessons as there are players, life lessons that will have been defined during their initial encounter.

### Life lessons

Al final de la aventura, si han salvado a Mowgli, tendrán que enseñarle una lección vital relacionada con los sucesos que han pasado durante la partida. Pueden ser cosas tan simples como «las abejas no te pican si te metes dentro del agua» o tan serías y complejas como «no hagas tratos con monos porque te intentarán engañar».

At the end of the adventure, if they have saved Mowgli, they will need to teach him a life lesson based on the events that took place during the game. This could be something as simple as "bees won't sting you if you get into the water," or something as serious and complex as "don't make deals with monkeys, because they will try to cheat you."

An important aspect is that Mowgli learns from the lessons provided by your players during each adventure, and he will not repeat the same mistakes in future adventures. These lessons will therefore shape how he behaves in future adventures; if he has been taught well, he will survive, but if he has not been properly instructed, he might die.

## Adventures

The adventures are quite simple: Mowgli has woken up and gone out to explore the jungle. He might encounter other animals, venture into an unknown part of the jungle, seek out food, or even found other humans like himself. In any case, you must ensure that Mowgli does not die, get lost, end up kidnapped, nor should he discover fire, while making sure he returns to the wolf den having learned something new and important for his future survival.

### Enter Shere Khan

At the start of each adventure, set a **30-minute countdown**. After 25 minutes, announce that roars can be heard. At 27 minutes, mention the sound of heavy footsteps nearby. At 29 minutes, the players will hear heavy breathing coming from the highgrass. Once 30 minutes have passed, if they have not yet solved the problem and returned to the wolf den with Mowgli, Shere Khan, the great tiger, will emerge from the shadows and devour the human cub.
